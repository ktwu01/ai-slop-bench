# Bravery club recap: evaluator notes

- Task ID: `bravery-club-recap`
- Rubric: `0.3`; grader policy: `strict-v1`

## Provenance

Content from `ktwu01.github.io` post `2026-09-17-why-i-dont-call-people-brave-zh.md`, whose argument the discussion follows: 近乎 is the operative word, fear enables courage rather than weakening it, courage is not judged by outcome, and the term carries moral approval. Failing fragments are **authored**.

## Target

Primary: **S6 fake-profound ending**. The subject is already philosophical, which is exactly the trap: a recap of a discussion about courage invites a closing sentence that performs wisdom about courage. The task asks for minutes of what was said.

Secondary: **fidelity**. Two questions were explicitly left unfinished. A recap that resolves them, or omits that they are open, fails.

## Required facts

- The line is 好学近乎知，力行近乎仁，知耻近乎勇 from 《礼记·中庸》.
- Most time went to 近乎: knowing shame can push toward courage but is not courage.
- Someone who acts without seeing danger is only bold; courage requires knowing what may be lost and that retreat is possible, and going forward anyway.
- Fear does not weaken courage; fear is what makes courage possible.
- Courage is not judged by outcome: success may be luck, failure does not erase the risk taken. A brave decision may still be a wrong decision.
- The word carries moral approval: someone taking great risk to harm others is called reckless or vicious, rarely brave.
- 耻 was read as moral self-awareness: seeing a gap between one's conduct and one's standards. External humiliation embarrasses; knowing shame requires admitting one did what one cannot endorse.
- Two questions were not finished: whether courage can be cultivated, and whether collective courage is the same as individual courage.

Do not invent attendee names, a vote, a conclusion on the open questions, a date for the next meeting, or additional citations.
