# Carry proposal summary: evaluator notes

- Task ID: `carry-proposal-summary`
- Rubric: `0.3`; grader policy: `strict-v1`

## Provenance

Facts from `ktwu01.github.io` commit `cdf17ac`, "Correcting the facts", which revised the YC Rebels post: the Carry proposal is about sharing carry with same-batch founders, and the plain-language gloss 简单说，他想重新分蛋糕 was restored. Failing fragments are **authored**.

## Target

Primary: **S5 and fidelity on someone else's dispute**. The material describes a conflict with a named person and a named organization. Framing an intro for a reading group invites taking a side, dramatizing the falling-out, or treating the proposal's merits as settled. The prompt states plainly that the proposal was not adopted, that accounts of the split differ, and that only his public statements are available.

Secondary: **S7 staged insight**, presenting an ordinary funding-model disagreement as a hidden truth or a reveal.

## Required facts

- Y Combinator is a Silicon Valley accelerator, founded 2005 by Paul Graham and partners.
- Its model: a management fee plus a share of investment returns, the share being called carry.
- Breck Yunits proposed that same-batch founders share that carry.
- His reason: same-batch founders create the long-term value, so they should share the returns.
- He publicly criticized the Hacker News algorithm for manipulating post visibility to favor YC-backed views and suppress dissent.
- Hacker News is operated by Y Combinator.
- He and YC later broke completely.
- The proposal was not adopted; accounts of the split differ; only his public statements are available.

Do not invent YC's response, dollar figures, dates for the split, other founders' positions, or an outcome for the proposal beyond non-adoption.

## Task fitness and length

A spoken intro for a reading group, 600 to 1000 characters, for listeners who have not read the source. Explaining carry is necessary. Balanced presentation is required by fidelity, but **do not require artificial neutrality**: reporting his stated reasoning is not endorsement.
